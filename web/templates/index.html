How it works

From your handwriting to a prediction

The hard part is not the network — it is making a photo look like the data the network was trained on. These five steps run on every image.

1

Capture

Your drawing or uploaded photo arrives as an ordinary colour image of any size.

2

Preprocess

Converted to grayscale and thresholded. Ink is detected whether it is dark on light paper or light on dark.

3

Centre

Cropped to the character, scaled into a 20-pixel box and centred by its centre of mass in a 28×28 frame.

4

Convolve

Stacked convolution filters detect edges, curves and stroke junctions, pooling into higher-level shape features.

5

Classify

A softmax layer turns those features into a probability for each of the 47 characters. The highest one is the prediction.

Technical details

Architecture

A VGG-style convolutional network: repeated Conv3×3 → BatchNorm → ReLU pairs, each block followed by MaxPool 2×2 and dropout, then a dense head with dropout and a 47-way softmax. Batch normalisation stabilises training; dropout and augmentation control overfitting.

Preprocessing

Otsu thresholding, polarity detection, largest-component isolation, bounding-box crop, aspect-preserving resize to a 20 px box, and centre-of-mass centring into 28×28 — matching how NIST built EMNIST. Pixels are scaled to [0, 1].

Training augmentation was limited to ±10° rotation and ±10% shift/zoom. Flips and large rotations are excluded because they change the label (b↔d, M↔W, 6↔9).

About

Handwritten Character Recognition

A computer-vision project that identifies handwritten characters from images using a convolutional neural network trained on EMNIST Balanced.

The system covers the full pipeline: dataset analysis, image preprocessing, model training and comparison, evaluation on a held-out test set, error analysis, and this inference interface.
